# Farnham Urban Sketchers & Friends

## Framing hints and tips

**Keep it simple\!**

### Use ready-made frames 

* **IKEA, Habitat, Wilko, The Range**. You can stain/paint the frames. Great Art do good ready-mades

* **Buy standard sizes** with easily removeable backs – that way you can swap artwork in/out of frame if it doesn’t sell

* **Choose simple materials** – you can paint or stain bare wood frames

* **Canvas paintings don’t need glass so cheaper to frame**

* **DIY the assembly** \- finish the back professionally with tape and d-rings/cord etc

* **Use a mount to resize** if your painting doesn’t fit the frame, you can use a custom-made mount (see below)

* **Upcycle old frames** – buy in charity shops, then paint or stain

### Mounts

A mountboard is a thick, rigid cardboard or foamboard.  It acts as a protective backing behind artwork or as a decorative border with a cutout window placed in front of the piece to separate it from the glass.

Mounted work looks great, if buying custom made mounts (measure carefully\!), cream/white is best so as to not to dominate artwork

Leave mounts a little larger on the bottom to improve illusion, use 5cms minimum. Larger mounts can look great also.

With masking tape, fix your painting to the back of the window.

### Other thoughts

When drawing or painting, leave a 2cm blank area around the sides (more for larger paintings). This way you can have the flexibility to overlay a bit of your painting to fit inside the frame or mount.

Experiment\! Try to narrow down the paper you use to a limited number of sizes (A4, A3 etc) then find those frames and mounts that work best with each size.

Clips, cord, hangings and backing board and take leaves a professional finish.  If the frame doesn’t already have receivers, you will need to attach them.  D-rings are a simple and cheap way to do this, screwing them into each side of the frame about one-third of the way down from the top.  (To prevent the wood of the frame from splitting when screwing in the D-ring, tap the sharp tip of the screw gently with a hammer to blunt it. This blunted tip cuts through the wood fibers rather than wedging them apart).

Choose or paint frames in a colour that is sympathetic to those of your painting.   The frame colour is often the one that appears only in small amounts in the painting, not the dominant colour.
